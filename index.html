Qiskit Fall Fest 2026 @ Palermo

Quantum events built by the community

Welcome to Qiskit Fall Fest 2026 @ Palermo, the city's community-led edition of Qiskit Fall Fest.

Supported by IBM Quantum and the Qiskit community, the event brings together students, researchers, professionals and anyone curious about quantum computing.

Back in Palermo. Following the 2023 edition, the Qiskit Fall Fest returns in 2026 in the city!

Whether you are taking your first steps in quantum computing or already work in the field, you will find a place to learn, exchange ideas, and build connections.

“

Qiskit Fall Fest is a global lineup of community-led quantum computing events, including

  • Hands-on workshops
  • Coding challenges and hackathons
  • Research talks and panels
  • Social and networking events

IBM Quantum partners with selected student leaders and volunteers each year to help plan and execute these events worldwide.

IBM Quantum

IBM's initiative for advancing quantum computing technology and making it broadly usable across research and industry.

IBM Quantum develops quantum computing hardware, software, and services for research and industry. Its work spans the full quantum computing stack and includes IBM Quantum Platform, which provides cloud access to quantum systems, learning resources, and development tools for a global community of organizations, researchers, educators, and developers.

ibm.com/quantum

Università degli Studi di Palermo (UNIPA)

A historic Italian university and a leading centre for education, research, and scientific culture.

With more than 40,000 members, the University of Palermo is one of Italy's largest universities. Founded in 1806 by Ferdinand III of Bourbon, it was authorized to award degrees in theology, medicine, law, and philosophy. The university has since undergone major transformations, becoming a centre for secular education and the home of many notable scientists. Its resilience, including during the period following the Second World War, has enabled it to adapt to changing educational needs while retaining an important role in the intellectual and political life of Sicily and Italy.

unipa.it

Dipartimento di Fisica e Chimica "Emilio Segrè" (DiFC) - UNIPA

The Department of Physics and Chemistry “Emilio Segrè” at UNIPA carrying forward Palermo's long-standing legacy in physics and chemistry.

The Department has a distinguished scientific legacy. Giuseppe Piazzi (1746–1826), the first director of the Observatory, discovered Ceres, originally classified as an asteroid and now considered a dwarf planet. The physicist Emilio Segrè (1905–1989), after whom the DiFC is named, discovered technetium, element 43, in Via Archirafi. In 1959, he received the Nobel Prize in Physics for the discovery of the antiproton. The chemist Stanislao Cannizzaro (1826–1910) achieved international recognition for proposing the Cannizzaro reaction.

The 2026 theme

A decade of quantum on the cloud

In 2016, IBM put the first quantum computer on the cloud, making real quantum hardware openly accessible. Ten years later, Qiskit Fall Fest 2026 celebrates a decade of IBM Quantum on the cloud: the evolution of its hardware, the discoveries it enabled and the communities that grew around it.

The birds in this year's visual identity refer to IBM Quantum processor families and roadmap architectures, whose names include Falcon, Hummingbird, Eagle, Osprey, Condor, Heron, and Flamingo.

Qiskit is a popular and performant software stack for quantum computing and algorithms research. You will be able to build, optimize and execute quantum workloads at scale.

Learn by building

Start with a quantum circuit

Qiskit Fall Fest is not only about listening: the programme includes practical sessions to explore quantum computing with the Qiskit SDK.

Try this Bell-state example, then find the sessions that match your experience in the programme.

Explore the programme →
bell_state.py 
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.primitives import StatevectorSampler

qc = QuantumCircuit(2)
qc.h(0)
qc.cx(0, 1)
qc.measure_all()

sampler = StatevectorSampler()
job = sampler.run([qc])
counts = job.result()[0].data.meas.get_counts()
print(counts)

Output

Quantum Computing 101: fundamentals, circuit model

Abstract

The postulates behind quantum computation - qubits, superposition, entanglement - and how the circuit model turns them into gates and measurements.

Quantum algorithms overview

Abstract

A tour of the algorithms that made quantum computing interesting, from Deutsch-Jozsa to Grover and Shor, and of what each one actually speeds up.

Qiskit 101

Abstract

A first hands-on contact with Qiskit: build, visualise, and run your own circuits on a simulator and on real IBM Quantum hardware.

Qiskit labs

Abstract

Guided Qiskit exercises of increasing difficulty, solved in small groups with the organizers available for support.
